## The short answer

If you need enquiries in the next few months and people already search for what you sell, start with **Google Ads**. If you can wait months for results and want a channel that keeps working when you stop paying for it, invest in **SEO**. Most businesses that rely on search end up doing both, because each makes the other cheaper.

## How they differ

| | Google Ads | SEO |
|---|---|---|
| **How you pay** | Per click, at a price set by an auction against other advertisers | In time and content: pages, fixes and articles, paid for up front |
| **How fast it works** | Ads can show as soon as Google approves them | Usually months, longer for competitive phrases |
| **When you stop** | Traffic stops when the budget stops | Rankings fade slowly, if at all |
| **What you learn** | Which searches turn into enquiries, quickly | Which questions your buyers research, over time |
| **Where you appear** | The ad slots above and below results | The organic results, and increasingly AI answers |

## Start with Google Ads when

- **You need enquiries this quarter.** SEO can't promise that timeline. Ads can, as long as people search for what you sell.
- **Your website is new.** A new site has no rankings and little authority, so SEO starts from zero. Ads don't depend on either.
- **You don't yet know which phrases bring customers.** Ads tell you within weeks which searches turn into enquiries. That is valuable before you spend months writing pages for the wrong phrases.
- **A customer is worth a lot to you.** If one client is worth thousands, paying a few dollars per click to reach people searching for exactly what you do is usually easy to justify.

## Start with SEO when

- **Clicks in your industry are very expensive** compared with what a customer is worth, so ads are hard to make pay.
- **Your buyers research for weeks** before they contact anyone, asking many questions along the way. Each question is a chance to be the page that answers it, and ads on informational searches rarely pay.
- **You have time.** If the business doesn't need new enquiries immediately, starting SEO early means it is working by the time you need it.

## Why they work better together

The search terms report in Google Ads shows the exact phrases people typed before they clicked, and which ones led to enquiries. That is the best keyword research available: it shows real buyer behaviour instead of estimates. Phrases that convert in ads are the ones worth building SEO pages for.

Over time it works the other way too. Once a page ranks well for a phrase, you can test lowering your ad bids on that phrase and see whether total enquiries hold. If they do, that budget can go somewhere it is needed more.

Both channels also need the same thing to convert: a landing page that answers the search. A visitor from an ad and a visitor from an organic result both leave if the page doesn't match what they were looking for.

## What about AI search?

Buyers now also ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews the same questions they used to type into a search box. These tools cite pages that answer questions clearly in text they can read. That is what good SEO produces anyway. Google [says its AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) have no extra requirements beyond normal SEO best practice.

## When neither comes first

Search only works if people search. If you sell something new that nobody knows to look for yet, there is no search demand to capture. Facebook and Instagram ads, which show your offer to people who weren't looking, usually come first in that case. Search comes later, once people know to look for you.

## The short version

Need results soon, and people search for what you sell: start with Google Ads, and use what it teaches you to plan SEO. Have time, expensive clicks, or buyers who research for weeks: start SEO now. Either way, send both kinds of visitor to a page that answers what they searched for.
