## 1. Check the tracking before you change the page

Before deciding a page doesn't convert, make sure enquiries are being counted at all. Broken tracking makes a working page look like a failure, and it is the cheapest problem to find.

- Submit the form yourself. Does the enquiry arrive in the inbox it should, and how quickly?
- Check that the submission shows up as a conversion in Google Ads or Meta Ads Manager.
- If people also call or message you, check whether those enquiries are counted anywhere. If they aren't, the page may be doing better than your reports say.

If the numbers are wrong, fix them first. Every later decision depends on them.

## 2. Does the page keep the ad's promise?

This is one of the most common causes. An ad makes one specific promise, for example "fixed-price 4-room renovation quote in 48 hours". The click then lands on a homepage that opens with "Welcome to our studio". The visitor now has to search for the promise themselves, and most won't bother.

Read your ad and your page side by side and check three things:

- **The headline repeats the ad's promise**, in close to the same words.
- **The offer is the same.** If the ad says "free quote", the page shouldn't lead with "book a consultation".
- **The audience is the same.** An ad aimed at landlords shouldn't land on a page written for first-time buyers.

If you run several ads with different promises, each one needs its own page. One page can't keep five different promises.

## 3. Is it asking for too much, too soon?

Every form field is a reason to leave. Ask only for what you need to send a useful first reply, usually a name, an email or phone number, and one question about what they need.

Match the size of the request to the size of the promise. "See our prices" is a small step, so asking for a 60-minute call straight away is a big jump. A short form or a price list fits it better.

## 4. Does it work on a phone?

Open your analytics and check what share of your ad clicks come from phones. For many campaigns, especially on Facebook and Instagram, it is most of them. Then use the page on your own phone, on mobile data, not Wi-Fi:

- How long does it take before you can read the headline? Run the page through [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) for a measured answer.
- Can you see what the offer is and how to act on it without scrolling far?
- Can you fill in the form with your thumb, without zooming or fighting the keyboard?

## 5. Is there proof near the decision?

Visitors from an ad don't know you yet. "Trusted by hundreds of clients" is not proof. Proof is specific and checkable: a review with a name, a result with a number and a timeframe, a client logo with permission, photos of real work.

Put it close to the form or button, where the visitor is deciding whether to trust you.

## 6. Is there one clear next step?

A landing page has one job. Full site menus, links to your blog, social icons and a second offer all give visitors a way out before they enquire. Remove what doesn't help the one decision the page exists for, and make the button say what happens next ("Get my quote", not "Submit").

## 7. Is the page getting the right visitors?

Sometimes the page is fine and the traffic is wrong. A page can't convert people who were never going to buy.

- **Google Ads:** open the search terms report and look at what people actually typed before clicking. Job-seekers, students and people looking for something free are common culprits. Negative keywords stop paying for them.
- **Facebook and Instagram:** check who is clicking. A broad audience can bring cheap clicks from people with no intent to buy.

## How to test a fix

Change one thing at a time, so you know what made the difference. Judge it on enquiries, not clicks or time on page, and give it enough traffic for the result to mean something. A handful of enquiries either way is noise.

If your traffic allows it, run an A/B test: show the old and the new version to visitors at the same time, and keep whichever produces more enquiries.

## The short version

Check the tracking, then the match between ad and page. Those two are the most common causes, and the cheapest to fix. After that, cut the form down, fix the phone experience, add real proof, and remove the exits. If it still doesn't convert, look at who is clicking.
