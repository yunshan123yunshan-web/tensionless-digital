Prompting Is Delegation,
Not Magic Words

A good prompt gives Claude everything needed to do the work without back-and-forth corrections. Think of it like briefing a smart colleague — you explain context, constraints, and what success looks like.

A weak prompt

"Make this email better."

A strong prompt

Adds context, tone, constraints, output format, and verification criteria.

Exercise

Rewrite "Act as an expert and make this email better" as a structured delegation prompt.

Include: role, context, task, constraints, output format.
