Delegation, Not Magic Words

A good prompt gives Claude everything needed to do the work without back-and-forth corrections.

Think of it like briefing a smart colleague — you explain context, constraints, and what success looks like.

A weak prompt

"Make this email better."

No context, no direction, no definition of "better."

A strong prompt

"I'm responding to a client who pushed back on our timeline. Maintain professionalism but show empathy. The tone should be confident without being defensive. Here's the draft…"

Mindset shift

Don't ask "What should I write?" Ask "Here's what I need — can you produce it?"
